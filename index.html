Cinette

A fast, private IPTV player for macOS and Linux.

Add your M3U playlist or Xtream Codes account and watch live TV, movies and series in one app. It's open source, with no accounts, ads or tracking.

Why Cinette

It's fast

The app is built in Rust with Tauri, which uses the web view your system already has instead of shipping its own copy of Chromium the way Electron apps do. Video plays through mpv with your computer's hardware decoder, and mpv draws it itself instead of handing it to the web page that holds the controls.

Playlists and TV guides are parsed in Rust and saved in a local database, and the app only downloads them again when they're due on the schedule you set. The channel list only draws the rows you can see, however long the playlist is.

It collects nothing

There are no accounts, ads, analytics or telemetry, and the developer gets no data from the app. Your playlists, logins, favorites and history stay on your computer.

You don't have to take that on trust. All of the code is on GitHub for anyone to read, every release is built from it by GitHub Actions, and the privacy policy lists each service the app talks to and what it sends.

You set it up your way

Choose how often each provider and its guide refresh. Pin, rename, reorder and create categories. Pick whether intros, recaps and credits are skipped automatically, with a button or not at all, and how far the back and forward buttons jump.

Set the default volume and hardware decoding, where downloads go, how many run at once and how long they're kept, and how early recordings start and how late they run.

Features

Your playlists

  • M3U and M3U+ playlists from a URL or a file, and Xtream Codes accounts
  • Several providers side by side, each refreshed on a schedule you choose
  • XMLTV TV guide (EPG), shown as a timeline of what's on for each live channel

Browsing

  • Separate tabs for live TV, movies and series, plus favorites, downloads and watch history
  • Search by channel or title, and on live TV by program name
  • Pin, rename, reorder and create categories, so a playlist with thousands of channels stays manageable

Playback

  • Plays nearly every IPTV stream format, including HLS, MPEG-TS, RTMP and RTSP, through mpv with hardware decoding
  • Reconnects on its own when the stream or your network drops, and picks up where it left off
  • Resumes movies and episodes where you stopped, with next episode and autoplay for series
  • Skips intros, recaps and credits with a button or automatically, using times from SkipDB and IntroDB
  • Keyboard shortcuts for playback, which ? in the player lists
  • Subtitles from OpenSubtitles, with timing and position adjustment
  • Keeps your screen awake while you watch

More

  • Download movies, episodes or whole seasons to watch offline
  • Record a live channel now, a program from the guide or any time range
  • Plot, cast and ratings for movies and series from OMDb
  • Optional category sorting with Google Gemini
  • Automatic updates

OpenSubtitles, OMDb and Gemini each need a free API key, which you add in Settings. Skip times need the OMDb key too.

Installing

macOS

Open the DMG and drag Cinette into Applications.

macOS says the app can't be opened or is damaged

Releases that aren't notarized by Apple are blocked by Gatekeeper the first time you open them. To open the app anyway, follow these steps.

  1. Try to open Cinette once and close the warning.
  2. Open System Settings and go to Privacy & Security. Scroll down and click Open Anyway next to the message about Cinette.
  3. Confirm with your password or Touch ID.

If macOS says the app is damaged, run this in Terminal and open it again. 

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Cinette.app

Linux AppImage

It includes libmpv, so there's nothing else to install.

chmod +x Cinette_amd64.AppImage
./Cinette_amd64.AppImage

The AppImage needs glibc 2.38 or later, which Ubuntu 24.04, Debian 13 and current Fedora releases have.

Debian and Ubuntu

sudo apt install ./Cinette_amd64.deb installs the app and the libraries it needs, including libmpv. It needs Debian 12, Ubuntu 24.04 or a later release.

Fedora

sudo dnf install ./Cinette_x86_64.rpm

Both packages need WebKitGTK 2.40 or later, which all of these releases have.

Ubuntu 22.04 and releases based on it, such as Linux Mint 21 and Pop!_OS 22.04, aren't supported. Their libmpv is too old for the app, and their glibc is too old for the AppImage.

Getting started

  1. Open Playlists and add a provider. That can be an M3U URL, an M3U file, or an Xtream Codes server with your username and password.

  2. If your playlist doesn't come with a TV guide, open the provider's settings and add an EPG (XMLTV) URL.

  3. Go to Channels and pick something to watch.

Cinette doesn't come with any channels or content. You need a playlist from a provider you're allowed to use.

FAQ

Does Cinette collect any data?

No. There are no accounts, analytics or telemetry. The app connects to the providers you add, to GitHub to check for updates, and to OMDb, OpenSubtitles or Google Gemini only if you add a key for them. When OMDb finds a title, the app also fetches extra ratings for it from whatson-api, and times to skip from SkipDB and IntroDB. Your playlists, favorites and history stay in a database on your computer.

Where does the app keep its data?

In ~/Library/Application Support/app.cinette on macOS and ~/.local/share/app.cinette on Linux. Downloads go to the downloads folder inside it. Before 0.8.0, these folders were named com.maxvideoplayer.app, and the first launch of 0.8.0 renames them.

Is this the app that was called Max Video Player?

Yes. Max Video Player was renamed Cinette, and updating keeps your playlists, favorites, settings and downloads. They move to the folders above.

On a Mac, an update from inside the app keeps the old file name, so the app is still called Max Video Player in Applications. You can rename it to Cinette there. If you install Cinette from the DMG instead, delete the old Max Video Player app. On Linux, installing the cinette package removes the max-video-player package.

A stream won't play

Check whether the same URL plays in mpv or VLC. If it does, open a bug report with your OS and app version. Leave out your playlist URL and login details.

The app won't open on my Mac after updating to 0.5.0 or 0.5.1

Versions 0.5.0 and 0.5.1 crash at launch, before they can check for updates. Install the latest version over the old one. Your playlists and settings are kept.
